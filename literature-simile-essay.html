Literature 11 · Writing

The Super Simile Essay

One essay, all fall, on one question: what do Dante's images of birds and flight mean?

Written in class, one paragraph at a time. The assignment, the rubric, and the model paragraph.

What you are building

By December you will have written a 7 to 8 page essay on Dante's bird and flight images, the longest thing most of you have ever written. You write it one paragraph at a time, in class, on the writing days listed below (about one a week). No single day asks you for more than one paragraph.

The birds carry the question of the course, desire, through all 100 cantos. In Hell the wings are grounded or twisted. In Purgatory they are retrained. In Paradise they arrive.

The rules

  1. You write ONLY in class, never at home. Your folder is the proof that every word is yours.
  2. Your folder stays in the room. I initial each paragraph the day you write it. No initials = not done.
  3. One paragraph per writing day, in about ten minutes, usually at the start of class. All three moves (below) in that one paragraph.
  4. Absent on a writing day? You write it at the start of the next class you attend. That is the make-up. A paragraph never written is a zero for that check-in.
  5. The first one came with help. On Thursday, September 3 we wrote paragraph 1 together and labeled the three moves as we went. Every paragraph after it has the same length and the same moves. You just write it on your own.

How to write it: the three moves, every time

Move 1 · Set About 2 sentences. Say where we are and what the image is, and name the canto.

e.g., "In Canto XIII Dante enters the wood of the suicides, where the Harpies, birds with human faces, nest in the trees and tear the leaves."

Move 2 · Quote One quoted phrase, framed. Set it up in your own words, quote a short phrase with the canto and line numbers, then explain it in your own words. A quote dropped in with no set-up and no explanation does not count as a quote.

Move 3 · Argue 3 or more sentences. Underline one sentence that makes a claim about MEANING: what the image shows about sin, desire, or grace. Then back it up.

"The Harpies are birds" is a fact. "The Harpies show flight twisted into predation" is a claim.

Length: 4 to 7 sentences. Ten minutes is enough if you did the reading and were in the seminar. The paragraph is always about an image we have just discussed.

The model paragraph

This is the paragraph we built together on Thursday, September 3, before you wrote your own. It uses the Charon simile from Canto III so that the birds of Canto V were left for you. The question for paragraph 1 was: What do the bird images in Canto V indicate about the nature of sin?

Set At the end of Canto III, Dante stands on the bank of the Acheron and watches the damned crowd toward Charon's boat. He expects them to hang back. Instead, they rush.

Quote Dante compares them first to leaves falling from a branch in autumn, and then to a bird answering its keeper (III.115–117). The souls do not have to be dragged into the boat. They go toward it the way a trained bird goes to a lure.

Argue Sin does not destroy desire; it aims desire at death. The damned still want something, and they still move toward what they want. What has changed in Hell is their target. A bird flies to the lure because the lure has been made to look like prey, and these souls fly to Charon because Hell has been made to look like what they chose in their life on earth. That is why nobody in this canto is forced. Dante wants us to see that the punishment is the wanting itself.

Count the moves: two sentences of scene, one quotation with the explanation after it, then the claim (underlined) and the sentences that back it up. Every paragraph this fall does the same three things.

Before you close the folder: five checks

  • Canto named
  • Thesis underlined
  • One quote, framed
  • About meaning, not plot
  • Legible

Five checks true = initialed. Anything missing = you fix it at the start of the next class.

Grading

The Super Simile Essay is one large grade in the Writing category, in two parts.

Check-ins, 40%. Every paragraph that earns initials on its day (or at your make-up) is full credit for that check-in. Started but not finished is half credit. A blank page or no attempt is a 0 for that day.

The finished essay, 60%. Graded once, in December, on this rubric:

The essay is graded onPointsWhat full points looks like
Thesis & argument40The essay makes ONE claim about flight and desire, and every image paragraph argues it. Each paragraph's own thesis is underlined and is a claim about meaning, not a fact.
Use of the text30Every image paragraph has one framed quotation: your set-up before it, the quoted phrase with canto and line, your explanation after it. No naked quotes.
Structure & unity20Paragraphs in order (Hell, Purgatory, Paradise), the Purgatorio section-intro in place, an intro with your main thesis, and a conclusion.
Completeness & care10Every initialed paragraph is present in the folder, the revisions from the fusion day are made, and it is legible.
Total100Graded once, after the December fusion day.
